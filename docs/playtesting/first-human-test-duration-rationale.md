# First Human Test Duration Rationale

The first human playtest uses **one scenario day**, from 08:00 through 22:00, rather than the full seven-day campaign.

This is a test-scope decision, not a new game mechanic. The purpose of the first human session is to determine whether the core Olive Drab experience works for people: command decisions, information limits, orders, movement, contact, control, communication, and GM operation.

## Why one day

A seven-day live campaign asks participants and the GM to sustain attention and continuity before the project has established that the basic experience is enjoyable. A one-day test gives the group a complete active cycle while keeping the commitment appropriate for a first human session.

The first test should still use the established 08:00–22:00 active window, flexible player attention, frozen-clock rule if the session reaches 22:00, and all existing order, contact, information, control, and safety procedures. The difference is that the human session ends after the Day 1 active window rather than beginning another scenario day.

## What remains outside the first human test

The seven-day campaign structure, overnight continuity, multi-day absence handling, and longer-term standing-behavior continuity remain documented prototype procedures and may be rehearsed separately. Their existence does not make them required evidence for the first human test.

Do not add new rules solely to fill the shorter session. The scenario, map, force representation, communication structure, and resolution procedures remain the same unless an existing inconsistency requires correction.

## Human-test question

The first human session should answer:

> Does one active day of Olive Drab produce a complete, understandable, and worthwhile command-game experience for the players and GM?

A later test can decide whether the experience should be extended into a multi-day or seven-day campaign.
