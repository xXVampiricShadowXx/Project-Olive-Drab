# First-test scope, acceptance, and post-test decision

Use this non-authoritative scope and evidence aid with the
[GM preparation route](index.md#prepare-in-this-order). The
[governing prototype sources](../reference/prototype/index.md) remain
authoritative. Scope, acceptance, rationale, and closeout remain separate
sections so a setup failure cannot be mistaken for a rules result.

## Quick start and acceptance

This is a navigation and acceptance aid for the existing first human test, not
a new scenario or rules authority. Use the complete
[Prototype Packet Index](09-prototype-packet-index.md) and its linked documents
to prepare and run the session. The packet index and
[Rule-Owner Decisions](18-open-rule-owner-decisions.md) page identify the
recorded clarifications and their related rule text. For a plain-language,
role-by-role guide for commanders, the GM, and observers, see the
[player playtest packet](../playtesting/player-playtest-packet.md).

### Quick start and acceptance: Test boundary

- **Time:** Day 1 only, 08:00–22:00. Close after the Day 1 final control-state
  check; the first test does not continue into another scenario day.
- **Place and objective:** Use the authoritative Brackenford 6-by-5 map and
  existing town-control objective.
- **People and units:** One company commander per side and one GM. Each company
  uses the existing company-to-echelon abstraction, with 1–4 subordinate
  echelon units represented by their force markers. Record the chosen count
  using the existing setup procedure.
- **Actions and adjudication:** Use the existing routine movement timings for
  eligible uncontested movement. The GM handles contact, contested actions,
  and rulings using the existing procedures.

The sources for these boundaries are the
[scenario](05-first-prototype-scenario.md),
[roles](04-game-master-and-player-roles.md),
[map](12-town-map-and-terrain-sectors.md),
[operating procedure](06-prototype-operating-procedure.md),
[timing aid](07-timing-and-action-aids.md),
[control and victory conditions](08-control-and-victory-conditions.md), and
[contact procedure](13-prototype-combat-and-contested-actions.md).

### Quick start and acceptance: Run the existing packet

1. Follow the [GM preparation route](index.md#prepare-in-this-order). Prepare from the
   [GM setup checklist](10-gm-setup-checklist.md); deliver the initial briefing
   using the [briefing template](14-initial-scenario-briefing-template.md);
   complete the [communication and order dry run](15-communication-and-order-dry-run.md)
   and [final preflight](16-final-gm-preflight-readiness-checklist.md).
2. During play, use the established order, report, timing, contact, and
   adjudication records. Apply no movement rate, contact outcome, or other
   procedure beyond the linked rules and aids.
3. At the Day 1 22:00 boundary, perform the final control-state check and close
   the session under the existing
   [end-of-scenario procedure](08-control-and-victory-conditions.md).

## Duration rationale

The first human playtest uses **one scenario day**, from 08:00 through 22:00, rather than the full seven-day campaign.

This is a test-scope decision, not a new game mechanic. The purpose of the first human session is to determine whether the core Olive Drab experience works for people: command decisions, information limits, orders, movement, contact, control, communication, and GM operation.

### Duration rationale: Why one day

A seven-day live campaign asks participants and the GM to sustain attention and continuity before the project has established that the basic experience is enjoyable. A one-day test gives the group a complete active cycle while keeping the commitment appropriate for a first human session.

The first test uses the established 08:00–22:00 active window, flexible player attention, and all existing order, contact, information, control, and safety procedures. The session ends after the Day 1 final control-state check rather than beginning another scenario day.

### Duration rationale: What remains outside the first human test

The seven-day campaign structure, overnight continuity, multi-day absence handling, and longer-term standing-behavior continuity remain documented prototype procedures and may be rehearsed separately. Their existence does not make them required evidence for the first human test.

Do not add new rules solely to fill the shorter session. The scenario, map, force representation, communication structure, and resolution procedures remain the same unless an existing inconsistency requires correction.

### Duration rationale: Human-test question

The first human session should answer:

> Does one active day of Olive Drab produce a complete, understandable, and worthwhile command-game experience for the players and GM?

A later test can decide whether the experience should be extended into a multi-day or seven-day campaign.
## Scope and post-test decision

### Scope and post-test decision: Explicit first-test scope

The first human test covers one neutral GM, one commander per side, one infantry
company per side, the six-by-five Brackenford map, written orders, reports,
contact/contested actions, standing behavior, map control, roleplay boundaries,
and one active scenario day from 08:00 through 22:00. **Observer participation
is optional.** When an observer participates, they may use the observer ledger and
closeout procedures without becoming part of command authority or the minimum
player arrangement.

The [duration rationale](#duration-rationale-what-remains-outside-the-first-human-test)
retains the boundary: close after the Day 1 final control-state check.
Overnight freeze and multi-day continuity remain broader prototype procedures
that may be rehearsed separately, not required evidence for this first session.

It does not test vehicles, air support, artillery, logistics, cyberwarfare,
national politics, detailed equipment, additional command levels, software,
competitive balance at scale, or a release-quality ruleset. It does not claim
that AI rehearsal results represent human enjoyment or behavior.

### Scope and post-test decision: Why the first test is one day

The first human session should establish whether the core experience is worth
continuing before asking participants and the GM to sustain a multi-day
campaign. A single active day provides a complete cycle of command, movement,
information, contact, control, communication, and GM operation while keeping the
participant commitment appropriate to a first test.

Do not add rules or features solely to fill a shorter session. Use the existing
prototype procedures and record anything that remains untested because the
scenario ends at Day 1.

### Scope and post-test decision: Post-test decision

Complete the report, observer closeout, and external setup notes before
choosing one outcome:

| Outcome | Use when | Next repository action |
|---|---|---|
| Rules-ready for human test | Scope was run and no blocking ambiguity remains | Preserve snapshot and schedule the next focused question |
| Rules revision required | A rule or document caused a repeatable blocker | Open a linked issue, revise narrowly, and rehearse again |
| Blocked by setup or recording failure | Channels, map, notifications, consent, or records failed | Fix external setup; do not infer a rules result |
| Inconclusive; repeat same test | Evidence is incomplete or a temporary ruling dominated | Keep scope unchanged and repeat with the same measures |

Use meaningful decisions, role participation, pace, channel friction, pause or
handoff use, accessibility, and consent observations as human-specific evidence.
Do not use a single participant preference as proof of balance.
